\section{Mathematical purpose}
Zipper algebras arise in the study of quotients of braid group algebras and
representations motivated by quantum topology. Bigelow introduced them in
\cite[\S8, pp.~297--298]{bigelow}, asking whether the three-strand slider
vanishes and which additional relations yield finite-dimensional quotients
(Questions~6 and~7). The Hecke and BMW quotients provide familiar points of
comparison. The research programme developed in \cite{thesis} constructs a
family of untwisting quotients and asks how their algebraic structure depends
on the untwisting polynomials.

The zipper algorithm and its finite-generation consequence give the broader
mathematical setting for this software. The computational questions include
whether one slider or untwisting relation follows from others, how presentations
change with the untwisting choice, and how to obtain explicit identities that
prove those dependencies. The package supports these questions through exact
noncommutative algebra, proof expansion, two-sided ideal certificates and
rational-function reconstruction. The original Gr\"obner-basis and cofactor
discovery used \texttt{f4ncgb}, by Heisinger and Hofstadler~\cite{f4ncgb}.

This reference first defines the algebras and states the relevant thesis result,
then specifies the implemented presentations and verification requirements and conclusions.
The geometric algorithm and its diagrams have their own exposition in
\cite[\S3.4]{thesis}.

\section{Ambient algebras and conventions}
Put $P=\Q[q,t]$ and $K=\Q(q,t)$, where $q,t$ are algebraically independent central
indeterminates. For $n\ge2$, use the free associative algebra
\[
 F_n=P\langle x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1},y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}\rangle.
\]
A word is an ordered tuple of indices in that displayed generator order,
starting at one. The empty tuple represents the unit. Concatenation denotes
multiplication, in the order written. In the braid group algebra, $x_j$ maps to
$\sigma_j$ and $y_j$ to $\sigma_j^{-1}$. Over a commutative coefficient ring
$R$, the corresponding braid group algebra is $R[B_n]$, with $q$ a unit when
using the untwisting hypotheses below.

The presentation explicitly includes the inverse relations $x_jy_j-1$ and
$y_jx_j-1$, adjacent braid relations and far commutations, together with
redundant inverse or mixed-sign versions used by the reduction engine. Let
$J_n$ be the ideal generated by the braid and inverse relations. Passing from
$F_n$ to $F_n/J_n$ identifies freely different words representing the same braid.
Consequently, an identity in the braid group algebra need not be a literal
free-algebra identity. The maintained verifier checks the latter. A certificate
using braid simplification must supply the corresponding braid/inverse terms
or use a separately specified group-algebra checker.

\section{Sliders, zipper algebras and untwisting}
We use the notation of \cite[Definitions~2.6, 2.9 and~3.1]{thesis}.
For the braid formulas below, expand each displayed abbreviation into its
specified signed word, then replace $\sigma_j$ by $x_j$ and $\sigma_j^{-1}$ by
$y_j$ to obtain the corresponding free-algebra polynomial used by the package.
This translation preserves word order and applies no braid or inverse
relations. The images in $R[B_n]$ and the chosen free-algebra representatives
must therefore be distinguished.

\subsection{Braid words and sliders}
Following the thesis's notation for fat crossings, put
\begin{align*}
 \varsigma_{k,j}&=\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}\cdots\sigma_{j+k-1},&
 \bar{\varsigma}_{k,j}&=\sigma_j^{-1}\sigma_{j+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{j+k-1}^{-1},\\
 \varsigma_{k,j}^{-1}&=\sigma_{j+k-1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_j^{-1},&
 \bar{\varsigma}_{k,j}^{-1}&=\sigma_{j+k-1}\cdots\sigma_j.
\end{align*}
Here $k$ counts crossings: the supported block has $k+1$ strands, with
$1\le j\le n-k$. For $k\ge2$, $\bar{\varsigma}_{k,j}$ and
$\varsigma_{k,j}^{-1}$ have different word orders.
For an $i$-strand slider starting at $j$, the recursion is
\begin{align*}
 X_{2,j}&=q\sigma_j^{-1}+(1-q)-\sigma_j,\\
 X_{i,j}&=(q^{i-1}\bar{\varsigma}_{i-1,j}-\varsigma_{i-1,j})X_{i-1,j}
       \quad(3\le i\le n),
\end{align*}
with $1\le j\le n-i+1$, and $X_i=X_{i,1}$. These are explicit elements of
the braid group algebra. Bigelow's barred indexed word denotes the inverse
of the entire indexed product. Translating that abbreviation gives the same
recursion \cite[p.~298]{bigelow}.

The package's slider dictionary is named \texttt{S}. Its entry
\texttt{S[(i,j)]} is the chosen free-algebra representative of $X_{i,j}$.
The principal translations are:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
Mathematical notation & Code representation \\
\hline
$\sigma_j$ & \texttt{xj}, generator index $j$ \\
$\sigma_j^{-1}$ & \texttt{yj}, generator index $n-1+j$ \\
$X_{i,j}$ & \texttt{S[(i,j)]} \\
$X_i=X_{i,1}$ & slider target \texttt{X\_i} \\
$\varsigma_{i-1,j}$ & ascending product \texttt{xj} through \texttt{x(j+i-2)} \\
$\bar{\varsigma}_{i-1,j}$ & ascending product \texttt{yj} through \texttt{y(j+i-2)}
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{The zipper algebra}
The level-two zipper relator is
\[
 Z_{2,j}=\bigl(q\sigma_{j+1}^{-1}+1-q-\sigma_{j+1}
             -q\bar{\varsigma}_{2,j}+\varsigma_{2,j}\bigr)X_{2,j}.
\]
For $i\ge3$, the higher zipper relator is
\[
 Z_{i,j}=\bigl(q^{i-1}\bar{\varsigma}_{i-1,j+1}-\varsigma_{i-1,j+1}
             -q^{i-1}\bar{\varsigma}_{i,j}+\varsigma_{i,j}\bigr)X_{i,j}.
\]
Here $3\le i\le n-1$ and $1\le j\le n-i$; for level two,
$1\le j\le n-2$. In particular, the two-strand formula is separate and includes
its scalar term $(1-q)X_{2,j}$. The package emits the chosen free-algebra
representatives of these relators.

The zipper algebra is
\[
 \mathcal Z_n(R,q)=R[B_n]/\langle Z_{i,1}:2\le i\le n-1\rangle.
\]
The builder also emits their shifted versions. These generate the same ideal:
conjugation by $\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{n-1}$ shifts each $\sigma_k$ to
$\sigma_{k+1}$ while $k\le n-2$, so successive conjugations translate each
supported block.

Imposing $X_2=0$ gives the Hecke quotient: indeed
$\sigma_1X_2=-(\sigma_1-1)(\sigma_1+q)$.
For $n\ge3$, the quotient of the zipper algebra by $X_3=0$ and
$(\sigma_1-t)X_2=0$ is the BMW braid-generator presentation described in
\cite[\S8]{bigelow} and \cite[\S3.2]{thesis}; see also \cite{birmanwenzl,murakami}.
The parameter comparison with the conventional BMW generators is discussed
below.

\subsection{The family of polynomial untwisting quotients}
The thesis defines the lasso full-twists by
\[
 \Theta_{k,j}=\varsigma_{k,j}\bar{\varsigma}_{k,j}^{-1},\qquad
 \Theta_2=\sigma_1,\qquad \Theta_i=\Theta_{i-1,1}\quad(i\ge3).
\]
Thus $\Theta_3=\sigma_1\sigma_2^2\sigma_1$. The first subscript of
$\Theta_{k,j}$ counts crossings in its two factors, while the subscript of
$\Theta_i$ denotes the strand count. Consequently, the shifted full-twist on
the support of $X_{i,j}$ is $\Theta_{i-1,j}$ for $i\ge3$.
For monic polynomials $p_2,\ldots,p_n\in R[\lambda]$ with unit constant terms,
form the two-sided quotient
\[
 \mathcal Z_n^{(p)}=
 \mathcal Z_n(R,q)/\langle p_2(\sigma_1)X_2,
                  p_3(\Theta_3)X_3,\ldots,p_n(\Theta_n)X_n\rangle.
\]
This is the class of algebras in \cite[Definition~2.9]{thesis}, allowing an
independent untwisting polynomial at every level.

\begin{thesistheorem}
With $q\in R^\times$ and the above monicity and unit-constant-term hypotheses,
$\mathcal Z_n^{(p)}$ is finitely generated as an $R$-module. In particular,
over a field it is finite-dimensional.
\end{thesistheorem}
This is the finite-generation formulation of \cite[Theorem~3.41, p.~62]{thesis}.
The theorem follows from the zipper algorithm's layered-zipper spanning result
(Theorem~3.39): there are finitely many combinatorial layer types up to twisting,
and the polynomial relations bound the remaining twist exponents. Monicity
reduces positive powers; a unit constant term also permits reduction of
negative powers. Thus the theorem supplies a family to study as the polynomials
vary. It does not assert that the resulting spanning set is a basis, determine
the dimension, or establish semisimplicity.

\subsection{Presentations implemented by the software}
The function \texttt{build\_relations\_ring} is in
\path{src/zipper_algebra/relations.py}; its front end is
\texttt{Presentation} in \path{src/zipper_algebra/presentation.py}.
A presentation records the strand count, an untwisting preset or explicit
per-level polynomials, powers, level limits, imposed zero sliders and named
additional relations. Its symbolic relation content is hashed; readable labels
alone do not identify an algebra. The Python constructor
\texttt{Presentation.from\_untwistings} accepts independent rational-coefficient
polynomials at selected levels. It emits $p_2(\sigma_j)X_{2,j}$ at level two and
$p_i(\Theta_{i-1,j})X_{i,j}$ at higher levels, including every valid shift.
An omitted level supplies no untwisting relation. The existing presets retain
their chosen free-algebra representatives and presentation hashes.

In the canonical untwisting presentation, the relations are the chosen
free-algebra representatives of
\[
 T_{2,j}=(\sigma_j-t)X_{2,j},\qquad
 T_{i,j}=\bigl(\bar{\varsigma}_{i-1,j}^{-1}
            -q^{i-1}\varsigma_{i-1,j}^{-1}\bigr)X_{i,j}\quad(i\ge3).
\]
In the braid quotient, left multiplication by $\varsigma_{i-1,j}$ converts
the higher relation to $(\Theta_{i-1,j}-q^{i-1})X_{i,j}=0$.
This equivalence uses invertibility; the emitted relation and this full-twist
form have different chosen representatives in the free algebra.

\subsection{Experimental parameter choices}
The builder also implements a level-two power relation $(\sigma_j^a-t)X_{2,j}$,
the squared relation $(\sigma_j-t)^2X_{2,j}$, and a historical option
$(\sigma_j^2-t^2)X_{2,j}$. The twist-power parameter affects level three only:
for exponent $m>1$, the emitted relation is the chosen representative of
$(\Theta_{2,j}^m-q^{2m})X_{3,j}$. At higher levels the canonical relation remains.
The established \texttt{Presentation} interface accepts rational coefficients;
the domain-bound interface below also accepts parameter-dependent coefficients.
Nonmonic inputs and zero constant terms are accepted as raw relations, without
normalization or a finiteness verdict. The theorem's unit hypotheses must be
checked in the selected coefficient domain, including after specialization.
Exact input and serialization examples are in
\path{docs/POLYNOMIAL_UNTWISTINGS.md} and \path{docs/PARAMETER_FIELDS.md}.
No structural theorem about a variant follows simply from choosing a flag.

The public presentation object rejects unknown untwisting choices, noninteger
strand counts and powers, level bounds outside $2,\ldots,n$, repeated additional
relation labels and targets requiring more strands than are present. Numeric
coefficients use either $\Q$ or $\Z/m\Z$. Calling the latter a field requires
the additional hypothesis that $m$ is prime; the coefficient backend does not
perform a primality proof. Nonunits cannot be inverted. The new field interface
below verifies positive characteristic separately.

\section{Exact parameter fields and domain-bound identities}
The classes in \path{src/zipper_algebra/fields.py} implement
$k(z_1,\ldots,z_r)$ for $k=\Q$ or a checked prime field, including $r=0$.
The ordered parameter list and characteristic belong to the domain. Sparse
polynomials have nonnegative exponent tuples of the declared arity. A field
element stores a numerator and nonzero denominator; equality is checked by
cross multiplication. No polynomial gcd or factorization is needed for this
equality test. Equal fractions may retain different literal representations.

Specialization evaluates a specified finite collection of stored expressions
where their denominators are defined and nonzero. Rational constants must also
be defined in the selected characteristic. This operation is not a map from
the entire function field to a fibre. For example, $(s^2-1)/(s-1)=s+1$ in
$\Q(s)$, but the former stored representative excludes $s=1$. Arithmetic can
change the representative and its recorded excluded locus, including by
cancellation to zero. Generic degree and dimension need not survive specialization.

The classes in \path{src/zipper_algebra/algebra.py} record an explicit field,
ordered alphabet and literal ordered relations. Even a zero free polynomial
retains its complete parent. The constructor
\texttt{AlgebraPresentation.zipper} substitutes the chosen $q$ into the existing
bare zipper emitter, then appends independent polynomial untwistings at all
valid shifts. No omitted level is inserted. The versioned JSON identity and
its SHA-256 record the field, alphabet and literal coefficients, including
denominator data. Equal fractions can conservatively give different literal
presentation identities.

A \texttt{CofactorIdentity} verifies
\[
 f=\sum_\nu L_\nu r_{i_\nu}R_\nu
\]
by exact free-algebra arithmetic over the declared field. Its factors may be
free polynomials, and its relation references are zero-based indices. Both
the parent and the full presentation hash are checked. A successful check
establishes membership over that field; it does not establish membership over
a smaller coefficient ring or at a pole. This JSON format is separate from
the established symbolic $q,t$ text-certificate format described below. Solver
production and reconstruction for arbitrary parameter fields remain further
integration work.

\subsection{Two-strand acceptance controls}
The following calculations give independently known answers for testing exact
cofactor and representation operations. They are small controls for the
parameter-field interface; the generic families remain the research setting.
Put $x=x_1$, $y=y_1$, $X=qy+(1-q)-x$, $r_0=xy-1$ and
$r_2=p(x)X$. For any polynomial $p$ with central coefficients, set
$F(x)=p(x)(x-1)(x+q)$. There is a literal free-algebra identity
\[
 F(x)=q\,p(x)r_0-xr_2.
\]
Modulo $xy=yx=1$, conversely $r_2=-yF(x)$. Thus the two-strand quotient is
$k[x,x^{-1}]/(F)$. If $F$ is monic of degree $N$ with nonzero constant term,
the words $1,x,\ldots,x^{N-1}$ span. The companion matrix of $F$, together with
its inverse, satisfies the defining relations, and these words applied to the
first coordinate vector give the coordinate basis. This proves independence
and hence dimension $N$. In particular, a monic degree-$d$ untwisting with
nonzero constant term gives dimension $d+2$ when $q\ne0$.

The executable examples in \path{examples/polynomial_quotients.py} check this
cofactor and cyclic representation. Over $\Q(q,a,b)$ they use
$p(x)=x^2+ax+b$ and the independently expanded polynomial
\[
 F(x)=x^4+(a+q-1)x^3+(b+a(q-1)-q)x^2
       +(b(q-1)-aq)x-bq,
\]
giving dimension four. A prime-field specialization is also checked. For
$q=2$ and $p=(x-u)^2$ over $\Q(u)$, the dimension is again four. Writing
$h=(x-1)(x+2)$, the element $(x-u)h$ is nonzero and square-zero: its square
is $Fh$, and the companion representation verifies nonvanishing.

Two exceptional-fibre examples distinguish different mechanisms. For $q=2$
and $p_s=sx^2+x+1$, the generic dimension is four, while at $s=0$ it is
three. Dividing by $s$ to obtain a generic monic polynomial would exclude
that fibre. For $p_s=x^2-s$ the generic dimension is four, but the fibre at
$s=0$ has dimension two: $x^2$ is invertible in the Laurent algebra, leaving
only $(x-1)(x+2)$. The examples certify removal of these invertible factors
using explicit inverse-relation cofactors. These conclusions use the exact
two-strand calculation, rather than assuming the full untwisting theorem's
hypotheses survive specialization.

\section{Complete Gr\"obner bases and certified normal forms}
Let $F=k\langle x_1,\ldots,x_m\rangle$, where $k$ is one of the exact fields
above, and let $I=(r_1,\ldots,r_s)$. The explicit verifier in
\path{verification/groebner.py} accepts a candidate family of nonzero
polynomials $h_1,\ldots,h_N$, bound to the full presentation identity. Each
$h_j$ carries a local identity of the form
\[
 h_j=\sum_\nu c_\nu u_\nu r_{i_\nu}v_\nu
       +\sum_\mu d_\mu a_\mu h_{\ell_\mu}b_\mu,
 \qquad c_\nu,d_\mu\in k,
\]
where all contexts are words. References to other candidate polynomials form
a directed graph. All references, including zero-coefficient references, are
validated, and the graph must be acyclic. Checking the displayed equalities
and inducting in dependency order proves $h_j\in I$. Shared subproofs need
not be expanded into cofactors of the original relations.

The order is fixed degree-lexicographic order, with lexicographic comparison
using the declared generator indices. Leading words are computed from the
polynomials, and nonzero leading coefficients are inverted in $k$. Every
resulting rewrite decreases this admissible order. The verifier checks that
each original $r_i$ reduces to zero, proving $I\subseteq(h_1,\ldots,h_N)$;
the derivations prove the reverse inclusion. It then reduces every inclusion
ambiguity, including equal leading words, and every proper nonempty
suffix-prefix overlap for all ordered pairs, including self-overlaps.
When every remainder vanishes, Bergman's Diamond Lemma
\cite[Theorem~1.2]{bergman} gives confluence. The irreducible words form a
$k$-basis of $F/I$, and reduction gives a unique normal form. Consequently,
a zero normal form proves membership in the original ideal and a nonzero
normal form proves nonmembership over the stated field.

Optional rule pruning uses leading-word divisibility to select reduction
rules. It does not omit validation of the derivation graph, reduction of the
original inputs or resolution of ambiguities for the retained rules.
A candidate polynomial proved to be a nonzero constant gives $I=F$ and
dimension zero.

Only after completion is established does the verifier enumerate irreducible
words. If this enumeration finishes, its length is $\dim_k(F/I)$. Exceeding
the word-count limit returns an undetermined dimension, not an infinity
verdict. Exceeding the rewrite/ambiguity work budget raises
\texttt{VerificationLimit}, without reporting a completed verification.
A returned \texttt{VerifiedReduction} retains immutable defining data;
later normal-form calls use the remaining work budget and reject elements
with a different parent, including zero elements.

The JSON schema \texttt{zipper-groebner-certificate-v1} stores the candidate
polynomials, local \texttt{ProofTerm} data and presentation hash. The numeric
f4ncgb decoder \texttt{certificate\_from\_solver} in
\path{verification/f4ncgb.py} supplies candidates, not a completion verdict.
The established discovery records continue to report
\texttt{basis\_complete=False}; their membership validation is separate from
this explicit completion operation. The four-strand example below has a separate local Singular producer.
The general production route below retains f4ncgb as its numerical producer.
GBNP's \texttt{IsGrobnerBasis} provides an
independent completion check for its supported fields; its quotient basis
and dimension functions require a genuine Gr\"obner basis \cite{gbnp}.
A basis check alone does not identify its ideal with the original defining
ideal. The API and resource limits are detailed in
\path{docs/GROEBNER_VERIFICATION.md}.

\subsection{Generic production from shared numerical proofs}
Let $K=\Q(a_1,\ldots,a_e)$ and fix a literal presentation over $K$.
A rational parameter assignment supplies a numerical f4ncgb computation.
Every local numerical derivation is checked against the actual specialized
relations. Zero specialized input rows are omitted from solver input, with an
explicit index map restoring their original positions before verification.
Neither this numerical check nor the solver's termination establishes a
generic dimension.

Process the shared proof graph in dependency order. For a proposed node, let
$C_1,\ldots,C_s$ be the contextual multiples of original relations or already
reconstructed parent nodes named by its numerical proof. For a candidate word
set $W$, form the matrix whose columns are the word-coefficient vectors of
\[
                  C_1,\ldots,C_s,\quad (-w)_{w\in W}.
\]
A vector $(u,v)$ belongs to this matrix kernel exactly when it gives the local identity
\[
               g=\sum_{w\in W}v_w w=\sum_{j=1}^s u_j C_j.
\]
Require the coefficient of the numerical leading word to be nonzero and
normalize it to one. Numerical tails are tried first; a failed support proposal
may be enlarged by contextual source words no larger than that leading word.
Larger words remain cancellation equations. If the kernel is underdetermined,
the observed monic node coefficients select a constant combination of generic
vector proposals whose evaluation agrees at the anchor. Candidate constant
combinations for this finite selection problem are reconstructed over $\Q$.

The scalar functions are reconstructed by FiniteFlow \cite{finiteflow}. Local
production uses candidate vectors without separately certifying their kernel
identity, rank or completeness. Only the ideal identities retained in the
finite-basis certificate are needed, and they are checked exactly at the final
algebra boundary. Reconstruction
returns a rewrite proposal with shared ideal derivations, not a dimension or
completion verdict. The separate certified matrix-kernel interface used for
invariants, minimal polynomials and cofactor discovery is unchanged.
The default production route constructs finite generator actions from this
proposal and runs the independent finite word-basis checker described below.
All raw proposal references and cycles are validated structurally before proof
pruning, including unused nodes and zero-coefficient edges. The finite
certificate retains the raw dependency closure of its action identities, or
of its unit identity in dimension zero. The unchanged checker validates every
retained local identity once, all spanning
identities, all original relations on the full representation, and cyclic
independence. Its acceptance therefore proves a statement about the actual
presented algebra over $K$, irrespective of how the numerical proposal was
selected. No separate Gr\"obner ambiguity check is needed for this conclusion.
Discarded unused proposal nodes carry no arithmetic correctness claim; the
separate rewrite proposal is unchanged.

An optional completion operation additionally checks both ideal inclusions and
all inclusion and overlap ambiguities. This establishes complete Gr\"obner
reduction, a distinct result. Production reports record
\path{word_basis_verified=True} after finite-basis certification and
\path{groebner_complete=None} when completion was not separately checked;
explicit completion instead records \path{groebner_complete=True}.
A failed proposal, pole, unsupported leading word or exhausted bound gives no
dimension verdict. There is no claim that one numerical proof shape always
lifts, or that the search is complete. The interface and retained evidence are
described in \path{docs/GENERIC_PRODUCTION.md}.

Publication exports and checks one saved JSON document and its decoded object
at a time. Its expected canonical-content hash is computed before a streamed
atomic write; the saved content must match that hash, and certificate parents
must match the saved presentation. This binds the saved data to the checked
objects while avoiding simultaneous serialized copies of the whole bundle.
The mathematical objects themselves remain in memory. Immutable output
generations, output byte hashes and source provenance are retained.

\section{Finite word-basis certificates and regular actions}
Let $A=F/I$, where $F=K\langle g_1,\ldots,g_m\rangle$ and
$I=(r_1,\ldots,r_s)$ over one of the declared exact fields. A finite word-basis
certificate supplies distinct words $B=(b_0=1,b_1,\ldots,b_{d-1})$ and sparse
matrices $M_1,\ldots,M_m\in\operatorname{Mat}_d(K)$. The column convention is
that column $j$ of $M_a$ gives the proposed coordinates of $g_ab_j$.

\begin{proposition}
Suppose explicit ideal-membership identities prove
\[
 g_ab_j-\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}(M_a)_{kj}b_k\in I
 \quad(1\le a\le m,\ 0\le j<d),
\]
and suppose that
\[
 r_i(M_1,\ldots,M_m)=0\quad(1\le i\le s),\qquad
 b_j(M_1,\ldots,M_m)e_0=e_j\quad(0\le j<d).
\]
Then the images of $B$ are a basis of $A$, $\dim_K A=d$, and the $M_a$ are
its left regular generator actions in this basis.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The span of the images of $B$ contains the unit and, by the ideal identities,
is stable under left multiplication by every generator. Induction on word
length proves spanning. The matrix relations give a representation
$\rho:A\to\operatorname{End}_K(K^d)$. If $\sum_jc_jb_j=0$ in $A$, applying
$\rho$ and evaluating on $e_0$ gives $\sum_jc_je_j=0$, so all $c_j=0$.
This proves independence. The certified column identities then identify the
matrices with the regular actions.
\end{proof}

A cyclic representation alone can describe a proper quotient; the spanning
identities supply the upper dimension bound. Spanning identities alone can
describe dependent words; the cyclic representation supplies independence.
Every defining matrix relation is checked on every coordinate vector, not only
on $e_0$. In dimension zero the separate requirement is an exact identity
$1\in I$; empty matrices give no such evidence by themselves.

The immutable \texttt{WordBasisCertificate} records the words, sparse generator
actions, action-node references and a shared \texttt{DerivationGraph}.
Local graph identities are checked on the full supplied acyclic graph, including raw
references with zero coefficients; zero polynomial nodes are allowed.
The JSON schemas are
\begin{itemize}
\item \path{zipper-word-basis-certificate-v1};
\item \path{zipper-generator-action-v1};
\item \path{zipper-derivation-graph-v1}.
\end{itemize}
Existing literal presentation hashes and Gr\"obner-certificate encodings are
preserved. A completed basis can help produce these witnesses, but completeness
is not a hypothesis of this finite-basis verification theorem.

The verifier \texttt{verify\_word\_basis} returns an immutable
\texttt{VerifiedFiniteAlgebra} with exact coordinates and products.
Generator actions remain sparse. Products are evaluated on demand from the
named basis words, without constructing a cubic-size multiplication table.
Matrix relation evaluation shares a noncommutative Horner graph so that
cancellation can occur before further matrix applications.
For a parameter field $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ with polynomial ring
$R=k_0[z_1,\ldots,z_r]$, the working evaluation writes each generator matrix
as $M_a=A_a/d_a$, where $A_a$ has polynomial entries and $d_a\in R$ is nonzero.
A Horner node representing $P_n(M)$ is assigned a common denominator $D_n$.
For a seed vector $U/S$, it evaluates the polynomial vector
\[
 V_n=D_nP_n(M)U,
 \qquad P_n(M)(U/S)=V_n/(D_nS).
\]
The $D_n$ clear the node's constant coefficient and the coefficients multiplying
$A_aV_{\mathrm{child}}/(d_aD_{\mathrm{child}})$. Their construction uses common
denominators and exact polynomial quotients before repeated matrix-vector
arithmetic. The inner evaluation then uses polynomial addition and
multiplication, avoiding a fraction gcd at each accumulation. Arbitrary
polynomial denominators are supported. All conclusions remain over $K$;
this computational clearing does not assert an $R$-basis or specialize at
zeros of the denominators.

Explicit operation, dimension and proof-node limits raise
\texttt{VerificationLimit} on exhaustion. Subsequent coordinate and product
calculations each have their own operation budget. The default finite-algebra
operation budget is $10^7$; the separate derivation-only checker retains its
$2\cdot10^6$ default.

Parameter-field computations use an internal normalized working representation
based on SymPy's sparse rational-function fields \cite{sympy}, installed through
the optional \texttt{symbolic} extra. Ground-field arithmetic needs no optional
dependency. This working arithmetic does not rewrite the literal input
coefficients or their presentation hash. A converted output coefficient may
have cancelled denominator factors and therefore its own conditions for any
specified coefficient evaluation. Definedness of the output coefficients alone
does not justify specialization of a quotient identity: its derivation may
invert further factors. The original literal denominator data remain part
of the input. API details and limits are in \path{docs/FINITE_ALGEBRAS.md}.

\subsection{Producing action certificates from terminating rewrite proposals}
A proposal orients each nonzero polynomial by its degree-lex leading word.
The producer enumerates every word irreducible under these leading words;
bounded enumeration must finish before a finite word basis is proposed.
Confluence is not assumed. Let $\rho(w)$ denote the result of the chosen
terminating reduction schedule, extended linearly to polynomials. If a proposed
rule is $g=\lambda(\ell-\sum_u c_u u)$ and $w=L\ell R$, then
\[
 w-\rho(w)
 =\lambda^{-1}LgR+
   \sum_u c_u\bigl(LuR-\rho(LuR)\bigr).
\]
Every child word is strictly smaller in degree-lex order. Memoizing these
identities gives an acyclic spanning proof without expanding shared ideal
proofs into original cofactor contexts. Applying this construction to each
$g_ab_j$ supplies generator columns and their proposed ideal identities.
Before removing unused nodes, the producer validates every raw reference and
cycle in the full proposal graph. It then retains exactly the raw dependency
closure of the generator-action proof roots, including zero-coefficient edges.
The independent word-basis verifier checks every retained shared proof identity,
all generator-column identities, all original matrix relations, and cyclic
independence exactly once. The proposition above then proves that the proposed
words are a basis of the presented algebra. A false retained derivation or an
inconsistent reduction proposal cannot establish a dimension. The zero algebra
instead retains the raw closure of a shared proof that $1\in I$.
The construction limit \texttt{max\_nodes} still bounds the full temporary
working graph before pruning. Removing unused derivations changes neither
the certified word basis nor its dimension. It makes no claim of shortest
basis words or a smallest or sparsest retained proof.
No cubic multiplication table or
separate completion check is required.

\subsection{Explicit cofactors from certified actions}
Let $h\in F$ and let $B$ be a certified finite word basis of $F/I$.
The general cofactor interface returns a representative $r\in\operatorname{span}_K B$
and an explicit identity
\[
 h-r=\sum_\nu c_\nu u_\nu r_{i_\nu}v_\nu
 \quad\hbox{in }F,
 \qquad c_\nu\in K,
\]
where every $r_{i_\nu}$ is an original ordered defining relation.
Thus $r=0$ gives an explicit ideal-membership cofactor for $h$; if $r\ne0$,
independence of $B$ proves that the class of $h$ is nonzero. The cofactor
concerns the difference $h-r$, not a nonzero quotient element itself.

The construction reduces suffixes through left generator actions. If
$w-\rho(w)\in I$ already has a recorded proof and
$\rho(w)=\sum_j a_jb_j$, then
\[
 gw-\sum_{i,j}a_j(M_g)_{ij}b_i
 =g\bigl(w-\rho(w)\bigr)
   +\sum_j a_j\left(gb_j-\sum_i(M_g)_{ij}b_i\right).
\]
Every summand uses an already retained ideal identity. This formula preserves
both noncommuting contexts and gives a shared proof of $h-r$ by induction.
In the zero algebra, the retained proof that $1\in I$ supplies a proof for every
$h$ by left multiplication.

Expansion of a selected acyclic proof node substitutes its references until
only original inputs remain, concatenating left contexts in outer-to-inner
order and right contexts in inner-to-outer order. Equal original-input contexts
are collected using exact coefficient arithmetic. The final explicit identity
is checked directly in the free algebra. This also applies to target-specific
proof graphs without acquiring a finite basis. Unrelated node polynomials need
no arithmetic truth claim for this selected-node conclusion, but all raw
references, parents and cycles are validated structurally.

The APIs \texttt{cofactor\_representation}, \texttt{expand\_derivation} and
\texttt{verify\_cofactor} in \path{cofactor/general.py} use the declared exact
field, including arbitrary parameter names and supported prime characteristics.
An expanded cofactor can be much larger than its shared graph. Bounds on
operations, graph nodes and retained context entries may therefore make
production inconclusive; no support-completeness or nonmembership claim follows.
The file-based example and precise bounds are documented in
\path{docs/GENERIC_PRODUCTION.md}.

\section{Symbolic q,t certificate data and exact conclusion}
Let $r_1,\ldots,r_s\in F_n$ be the ordered defining relations of a specified
presentation. A certificate for $f\in F_n$ is a finite list of terms
$(u_\nu,i_\nu,v_\nu,N_\nu,e_\nu)$, together with an ordered family of nonzero
polynomials $a_1,\ldots,a_h\in\Z[q,t]$. Here $u_\nu,v_\nu$ are words,
$i_\nu$ names an actual relation, $N_\nu\in P$, and
$e_\nu\in\mathbb N^h$. The intended identity is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
 f=\sum_\nu\frac{N_\nu}{D_\nu}u_\nu r_{i_\nu}v_\nu,
 \qquad D_\nu=\prod_{k=1}^h a_k^{e_{\nu k}},
\end{equation}
in $K\langle x_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}\rangle$. Atom irreducibility is unnecessary
for checking this identity; it is relevant to reconstruction efficiency.
The atom search bounds do not limit the exponents in a composed certificate.

Define $E_k=\max_\nu e_{\nu k}$, with zero maxima for an empty certificate,
and $M=\prod_k a_k^{E_k}$. Since $P$ is an integral domain and each atom is
nonzero, $M\ne0$. The exact checker compares the polynomials
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clear}
 Mf\quad\text{and}\quad
 \sum_\nu N_\nu\left(\prod_k a_k^{E_k-e_{\nu k}}\right)
                   u_\nu r_{i_\nu}v_\nu.
\end{equation}
Equality proves \eqref{eq:cert} and hence membership of $f$ in the two-sided
ideal generated by the relations over $K$. It also proves membership in the
corresponding localization of $P$, inverting the used atoms. A specialization
of the rational identity is available wherever the used polynomial factors are
nonzero. Atom names in a serialized certificate are formal labels; their explicit
polynomial definitions determine the specialization condition. Verification
reports display those polynomial factors. The polynomial identity \eqref{eq:clear} remains meaningful at their
zeros, but need not imply the specialized membership statement there.

The certificate boundary rejects zero atoms, nonintegral atom coefficients,
negative or noninteger polynomial exponents, denominator vectors of the wrong
length, inexact numerical coefficients, out-of-range word indices and unknown
relation labels. Both verifiers validate the supplied presentation identity.
Objects are revalidated at the checking boundary because their data are mutable.
A malformed certificate raises a validation error. A well-formed false identity
returns a report with \texttt{pass=False}.

Legacy files without a presentation hash require an explicitly supplied
presentation. Their historical origin does not replace verification against
those actual relations. New certificates should retain the presentation hash
and explicit target. Named sliders, additional named targets and direct
polynomial targets share the same validated target resolution.

\section{Independent polynomial identity testing}
The second checker evaluates the cleared identity on a product grid of distinct
rational integers. For each parameter, its bound includes both the target
degree plus the clearing-factor degree and the degrees of every cofactor term.
If the bounds are $(d_q,d_t)$, a grid with $(d_q+1)(d_t+1)$ points suffices.

\begin{proposition}
If a polynomial in $\Q[q,t]$ of bidegree at most $(d_q,d_t)$ vanishes on such a
product grid, it is zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fix a grid value of $t$ and apply the univariate root bound in $q$. Each
coefficient in $q$ then vanishes at all $d_t+1$ values of $t$, so the same
root bound makes each coefficient zero. Apply this to every free-word coefficient.
\end{proof}

The checker evaluates the polynomial product $M/D_\nu$ directly, rather than
dividing its evaluations. Thus grid points at poles cause no undefined
division. Negative numerator exponents are rejected: a Laurent polynomial
cannot use this polynomial degree bound without additional clearing.
This exact finite-grid argument differs from checking a few selected parameter
values. Its arithmetic is separate from the symbolic accumulation, but it
shares the presentation builder, input validation and target resolution.

\section{Proof expansion and contextual composition}
A basis proof is an acyclic directed graph expressing each basis element as a
sum of contextual multiples of input relations and other basis elements.
Forward references are allowed; cycles and missing dependencies are errors.
Membership validation checks the local polynomial identities in dependency
order, without expanding shared subproofs. Explicit cofactor composition may
subsequently expand these derivations. Substitution respects multiplication order. If
\[
 g_k=\sum_\nu c_{k\nu}L_{k\nu}r_{i_{k\nu}}R_{k\nu},
 \qquad f=\sum_s m_s u_sg_{k_s}v_s,
\]
composition produces terms with coefficient $m_sc_{k_s\nu}$ and contexts
\[
          (u_sL_{k_s\nu},\ i_{k_s\nu},\ R_{k_s\nu}v_s).
\]
No commutation or cancellation of free words is implicit. Repeated uses of one
basis element remain separate until their full contexts agree and coefficients
can be added. The number of multipliers must equal the number of skeleton steps,
and an explicit nonzero skeleton remainder precludes membership composition.

For sampled multipliers, each sample reduction must have the same ordered
sequence of $(u_s,k_s,v_s)$ as the anchor, where $k_s$ is the leading-word key
rather than the engine's changing numerical index. The data retain one
multiplier per occurrence. A sample with a different contextual schedule is
excluded. A successful fit is a candidate; the resulting identity still needs
exact verification.

\section{Exact reconstruction of candidates}
\subsection{Standalone coefficient fitting}
A sample consists of a distinct parameter point in $\Q^2$ and an exact rational
value. Repeated locations count once; conflicting values and floating-point
coefficients are rejected. For a fixed nonzero denominator proposal and a
numerator bidegree bound, interpolation uses the available fitting rows and
requires full column rank. A consistent rank-deficient system does not determine
a unique numerator and is not completed by arbitrarily setting free variables
to zero. Numerator coefficients belong to $\Q$, rather than necessarily $\Z$.

Univariate rational reconstruction fixes a monic denominator, solves the exact
linear system, cancels a polynomial greatest common divisor and normalizes the
result. The reduced denominator must be nonzero at every supplied point, and the
candidate must match every fitting and held-out value. This prevents a spurious
cross-multiplied equation $0=0$ from concealing an incorrect sample at a pole.

In the standalone fitting API the factor family is proposed from fitting data, starting without
predeclared denominator factors. Exact univariate factorization and lifting
across parameter lines are tried before bounded polynomial enumeration.
The current line-lifting budget allows degree at most four in the other
parameter. Varying-factor lifting uses at least five distinct parallel coordinates
and reserves two for validation, subject to the requested larger minimum.
Repeated univariate factors also require at least five distinct parallel
coordinates. These are inference heuristics.
The search has explicit finite budgets and can leave unresolved line factors;
it does not certify that every generic pole has been found. An explicitly
supplied ordered factor family remains a user-selected search constraint.

Held-out locations are reserved before fitting and denominator inference. Their
coefficients do not participate in inference of the factor family. A passing
held-out check is additional evidence, not a rational-function identity proof.
Line restrictions can cause distinct polynomial factors to coincide, so inferred
exponents are heuristic proposals. They are validated and have a general-search
fallback. For arbitrary declared factors, absence of a cancellable whole factor
does not imply coprimality of the numerator and denominator; stronger assertions
require irreducible, pairwise nonassociate factors.

Failed predictions, an unavailable common reduction shape and exhaustion of the
chosen degree or factor bounds do not show that more data cannot help. The standalone fitting routine reports an inconclusive
outcome when it lacks a candidate in its search class. In particular, reducing to a nonzero
remainder against an uncertified basis establishes no nonmembership conclusion,
even at that specialization. The exception \texttt{NeedMoreSamples} reports an
inconclusive reconstruction. Candidate reconstruction is characteristic zero.

\subsection{Discovery from contextual input relations}
The installed discovery command obtains a candidate basis and a nonexpanded
proof graph at a rational anchor. Every numerical basis identity is checked
against the actual specialized defining relations. Reducing the target records
its ordered contextual uses of the candidate basis. Expanding the relevant
proof graph propagates left and right word contexts to the original input
relations. Numerical proof coefficients are discarded in this step: they
supply neither the generic coefficients nor denominator proposals.

Let the resulting distinct triples be $(u_j,i_j,v_j)$, for $1\le j\le c$,
where $r_{i_j}$ is an original defining relation over $K=\Q(q,t)$. Take the
union of all words occurring in $u_jr_{i_j}v_j$ and the target $h$, and fix an
order on that finite set. Relative to that free-word basis, let $A$ have
columns $u_jr_{i_j}v_j$, let $b$ be the coordinate column of $h$, and put
\[
 M=[A\mid -b].
\]
FiniteFlow reconstructs a complete right kernel $N$ of $M$, which is then
verified independently as described below. If a column $(z,s)$ of $N$ has
$s\ne0$, then $Az=bs$, hence
\[
 h=\sum_{j=1}^{c}\frac{z_j}{s}\,u_jr_{i_j}v_j
 \quad\text{in }K\langle\mathcal G\rangle.
\]
A separate free-algebra checker expands the original relations with these
coefficients and checks the complete identity before publication. Thus even
an error in support construction or matrix assembly cannot be accepted solely
because the matrix calculation succeeds.

The anchor graph proposes a finite support; it does not prove that this support
suffices generically. A coefficient that vanishes at the anchor can hide a
needed context. If every kernel vector has final coordinate zero, this support
cannot represent the target, but membership in the full defining ideal remains
unresolved. A nonzero target remainder against an uncertified basis likewise
establishes no nonmembership result. Support-size, exact-product, reconstruction
degree and prime budgets yield an inconclusive outcome upon exhaustion; they
are computational limits rather than hypotheses of an accepted identity.
The command continues to report \texttt{basis\_complete=False}.

\subsection{Finite-field reconstruction of matrix kernels}
Let $K=\Q(t_1,\ldots,t_s)$, let $M\in K^{m\times n}$, and fix the ordered
parameters of $K$. FiniteFlow~\cite{finiteflow} produces a candidate kernel by
solving specialized finite-field systems and reconstructing their rational
coefficients. This computation is separate from generating noncommutative word
contexts or proving that a matrix is the regular action of an algebra.

A kernel certificate supplies $N\in K^{n\times(n-r)}$, a list $J$ of $n-r$
distinct row indices, and an $r\times r$ minor $B$ of the original matrix.
It includes a prime $p$, a parameter point $a\in\mathbb F_p^s$, and an inverse
$C\in\operatorname{Mat}_r(\mathbb F_p)$ for $B(a)$. Every stored input
coefficient used in $B(a)$ must be defined under this specialization: rational
constant denominators and evaluated polynomial denominators must be nonzero.
The independent verifier checks
\[
 MN=0,\qquad N_{J,*}=I_{n-r}\quad\text{over }K,
 \qquad C B(a)=I_r\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_p.
\]
The first two identities imply $\operatorname{rank}_K M\le r$, since the
columns of $N$ are independent. The last identity implies that the determinant
of $B$ is a nonzero rational function and hence
$\operatorname{rank}_K M\ge r$. Together they prove the exact generic rank
and a basis of the right kernel. Empty identity blocks have their usual
meaning for ranks zero and $n$.

The matrix identity binds the witness to the supplied input. It does not by
itself establish how that matrix was obtained from a presentation; that source
requires its own finite-basis, action or cofactor checks. Sample agreement,
a library success status, and a chosen reconstruction degree bound do not
replace these identities. Unlucky primes, points, or learned pivot choices can
cause failure or an incorrect candidate; a candidate is accepted only after
all exact checks pass. Coefficient bounds, prime budgets and thread counts are
computation settings rather than hypotheses of the conclusion.

\subsection{Minimal polynomials on cyclic algebra orbits}
Let $A$ be a verified finite-dimensional unital associative algebra over $K$,
of dimension $D$, with its certified ordered word basis. For $a,v\in A$,
consider the cyclic sequence
\[
 v,\ av,\ a^2v,\ldots.
\]
Its annihilator in $K[z]$ has a unique monic generator $m_{a,v}$.
The default $v=1$ gives the minimal polynomial of $a$, equivalently that of
left multiplication by $a$ on the whole algebra. A general seed concerns only
its cyclic orbit; $m_{a,v}(a)v=0$ need not imply $m_{a,v}(a)=0$.
For $v=0$, including the zero algebra, the generator is $1$, of degree zero.

For a claimed monic polynomial $c_0+c_1z+\cdots+z^d$, form the actual
coordinate matrix
\[
 C_d=[\,v\mid av\mid\cdots\mid a^dv\,]\in K^{D\times(d+1)}.
\]
The checker recomputes these columns from the verified algebra. It verifies
a complete kernel certificate of rank $d$ and nullity one, and compares
the supplied monic coefficient vector with that kernel vector after
normalization. Thus it proves
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^d c_j a^jv=0,\qquad c_d=1,\qquad
 \operatorname{rank}_K C_d=d.
\]
The relation puts the last column in the span of the preceding $d$ columns.
Consequently those columns are independent, and no lower-degree polynomial
annihilates $v$. The polynomial is therefore the exact relative minimal
polynomial, not merely an annihilator.

The producer uses the maintained FiniteFlow kernel interface~\cite{finiteflow}.
Prefix lengths grow geometrically. If the first dependent vector has index
$d$, applying left multiplication by $a$ to its relation shows that the span
of the preceding vectors is invariant. Every longer prefix has rank $d$.
An exact rank from a dependent prefix therefore determines the smaller
prefix to certify. The calculation uses generator actions on vectors and
does not materialize every basis multiplication matrix.

Production of nonzero orbits currently uses characteristic-zero coefficient
fields. The independent verifier accepts the supported exact fields and
minor witnesses of the kernel interface. Degree bounds are capped by $D$.
An exhausted search returns no polynomial. Operation limits apply per
multiplication and product limits per kernel verification; they do not bound
process memory or elapsed time.

\section{A four-strand square-zero quotient over \texorpdfstring{$\Q(q,t)$}{Q(q,t)}}
Let $A$ be the four-strand zipper quotient over $k=\Q(q,t)$ with polynomial
untwistings
\[
 p_2(\lambda)=(\lambda-t)^2,\qquad
 p_3(\lambda)=\lambda-q^2,\qquad
 p_4(\lambda)=\lambda-q^3,
\]
at every valid shift. Thus the two-strand untwisting relation is
$(x_j-t)^2X_{2,j}=0$, and the higher untwistings are canonical. The polynomials are monic with unit constant terms in $k$, so the
finite-generation theorem above applies. The literal presentation contains
31 ordered relations, including the canonical
four-strand relation $T_4$ in its chosen free-algebra representative.
The example constructs \texttt{Presentation(4, "square-zero")} and converts
its coefficients to $k$. It therefore uses the canonical higher relations
$T_3,T_4$, rather than the literal full-twist polynomial representatives
emitted by \texttt{from\_untwistings}. Inverse relations identify the ideals
of these representatives by invertible left factors, but the free polynomials
and literal presentation identities differ.
The following are generic conclusions over $k$:
\[
 \dim_k A=186,\qquad X_3=0,\qquad
 w=(x_1-t)X_{2,1}\ne0,\qquad wAw=0.
\]
In particular, $J=AwA$ is a nonzero two-sided ideal with
$J^2=A(wAw)A=0$. Hence $A$ is not semisimple: the nonzero square-zero ideal $J$ supplies
the obstruction.

The certificate records 299 auxiliary input-derived polynomials and
148 candidate basis polynomials: 447 nodes with explicit local derivations.
The GAP checker requires every input reference in the entire graph to lie
among the first 30 relations, excluding $T_4$. The local identities therefore
prove that the candidate ideal lies in the ideal of those 30 inputs.
Independently, reduction of all 31 input relations to zero proves the
reverse inclusion, and the critical-ambiguity checks establish completion of
the 148-polynomial basis. Enumeration gives 186 irreducible words. The
nonzero normal form of $w$ proves nonvanishing; the exact normal forms of
$wbw$ vanish for all 186 basis words $b$, proving $wAw=0$ by linearity.
The normal forms of $X_3$ and $T_4$ also vanish.

Candidate production uses Singular's letterplace computations
\cite{singular}; the derivation, completion, dimension and ideal checks use
GAP 4.15.1 with GBNP 1.1.0 independently \cite{gbnp}. The input-reference
bound and vanishing normal form of $T_4$ together prove its redundancy.
The full 447-node Python Gr\"obner verification did not finish in a
60-second local probe; that completion route uses Singular and GAP.
A separate finite word-basis certificate extends those 447 nodes with
1,116 generator-column identities. The maintained Python finite-basis verifier
checked all 1,563 nodes, the 5,766 original matrix-relation columns and
186 cyclic-coordinate identities, independently certifying dimension 186
and the sparse regular generator actions without a completion hypothesis.
Its six generator matrices have 14,511 nonzero entries. Commands and the
recorded verification counts are in \path{docs/FINITE_ALGEBRAS.md}. This result concerns the generic parameter field, with no
specialization involved. Commands, source roles and generated proof data are
described in \path{docs/FOUR_STRAND_SQUARE_ZERO.md}.

\section{Research results and computational evidence}
The linear untwisting case connects the family above to the BMW quotient.
The thesis exhibits an explicit two-sided identity for $X_3$ using the zipper,

 m=q^{-1/2}(1-q),\qquad l=q^{1/2}t^{-1},\qquad
 G_i\longmapsto q^{-1/2}\sigma_i,\quad
 E_i\longmapsto(1-q)^{-1}X_{2,i}.
\]
This scalar extension is part of that parameter comparison.

The current free-algebra certificate example and the thesis's compact

The optional GAP exporter produces a standalone identity in the free associative
