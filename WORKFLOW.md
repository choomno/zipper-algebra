## 1. Mathematical setting

### 1.1 The zipper algebra

Fix a strand count $n$ and the coefficient ring $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$. The **zipper algebra** $\mathcal{Z}_n$ is the quotient of the free $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$-algebra on $2(n-1)$ generators $\{\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_{n-1}, \sigma_1^{-1}, \ldots, \sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\}$ by the two-sided ideal generated by:

1. **Invertibility:** $\sigma_i \sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_i^{-1}\sigma_i = 1$ for each $i$.
2. **Braid relations:** $\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$, and $\sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i$ for $|i-j| \geq 2$. We also include the $y$-mirror and four mixed-sign length-3 relators ($\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i^{-1} = \sigma_{i+1}^{-1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1}$, etc.) explicitly to shorten reduction chains.
3. **Sliders.** Define $S[2][j] := q \sigma_j^{-1} + (1-q) - \sigma_j$ for $1 \le j \le n-1$, and recursively
$$S[i][j] \;:=\; \bigl(q^{i-1}\, \sigma_j^{-1} \sigma_{j+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{j+i-2}^{-1} \;-\; \sigma_j \sigma_{j+1} \cdots \sigma_{j+i-2}\bigr)\, S[i-1][j].$$
4. **Zipper relations** for each consecutive-strand slider $S[i][j]$ with $j+i \le n$.
5. **Bigelow untwisting relations:**
$$(\sigma_j - t)\,S[2][j] = 0, \qquad \bigl(\sigma_{j+i-2}\sigma_{j+i-3}\cdots\sigma_j - q^{i-1}\,\sigma_{j+i-2}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_j^{-1}\bigr)\,S[i][j] = 0 \;\; (i\ge 3).$$

The conjecture-of-record (proved abstractly by dimension counting) is $\mathcal{Z}_n \cong \mathrm{BMW}_n$. Both algebras are free $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$-modules of rank $(2n-1)!!$, so the existence of a surjective algebra map followed by a dimension match forces an isomorphism. As a corollary, every element of $\mathrm{BMW}_n$ that vanishes on the BMW side must already be zero on the zipper side; in particular, the $n$-strand slider $X_n := S[n][1]$ vanishes in $\mathcal{Z}_n$ for every $n$, and likewise for the lower sliders $X_k = S[k][1]$ inside $\mathcal{Z}_n$ for $n > k$.

### 1.2 The target identity at $n=4$

The dimension argument gives existence of these vanishing identities but not the explicit witnesses, and the witnesses are what one needs for a constructive proof. For $n=4$ we have $(2\cdot 4-1)!! = 105$, and the smallest non-trivial sliding identity is
$$X_3 \;=\; S[3][1] \;=\; 0 \quad \text{in } \mathcal{Z}_4.$$
This document records the explicit two-sided cofactor decomposition proving that identity.

### 1.3 Two-sided cofactor representations

If $I = \langle f_1, \ldots, f_m\rangle$ is a two-sided ideal in a free associative algebra $A = R\langle x_1, \ldots, x_v\rangle$ and $g \in I$, the most general statement of membership is
$$g \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{N} c_k\, L_k\, f_{i_k}\, R_k, \qquad c_k \in R,\; L_k, R_k \in A,$$
i.e. a finite linear combination of two-sided multiples of the generators. The four-tuples $(c_k, L_k, i_k, R_k)$ collectively form the **cofactor representation** or **proof of membership**. Both the left and right multipliers are required because the ideal is two-sided; in the free algebra these are independent, and one cannot in general absorb $R_k$ into $L_k$ or replace the two-sided sum with a one-sided one.

Unlike the commutative case, the cofactor representation is **never unique**: the set of all valid $(c_k, L_k, i_k, R_k)$ tuples summing to $g$ forms an affine subset of $A^{\,4N}$ whose direction is the kernel of the bilinear evaluation map, called the **syzygy module** of $\{f_1, \ldots, f_m\}$. Two cofactors of the same $g$ differ by an element of this module. This non-uniqueness drives both the strategy (Phase 3 below) and the failure mode of the naive approach (§3 below).

## 2. Computational background

### 2.1 Noncommutative Gröbner bases

A **monomial order** on $A$ is a well-order of the monomial monoid (= free monoid on $\{x_1, \ldots, x_v\}$) compatible with left and right multiplication. The standard choice for our problem is *degree-lexicographic with* $x_1 < x_2 < \cdots < x_{v}$: order monomials first by total length, then lexicographically. This is a well-order because there are finitely many monomials below any given degree.

Given an order, every nonzero polynomial $f \in A$ has a **leading monomial** $\mathrm{LM}(f)$ and **leading coefficient** $\mathrm{LC}(f)$. Divisibility in the noncommutative monoid is *substring containment*: a monomial $\mu = x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n}$ is divisible by $\lambda$ iff $\lambda$ occurs as a contiguous substring of $\mu$. When $\mu = u\,\lambda\,v$ for monomials $u, v$, the **one-step reduction** of $f$ by $g$ (with $\mathrm{LC}(g) = c$, $\mathrm{LM}(g) = \lambda$, $\mathrm{LM}(f) = \mu$, $\mathrm{LC}(f) = d$) is
$$f \;\longrightarrow\; f - \tfrac{d}{c}\, u\, g\, v.$$
A polynomial in which the leading monomial cannot be reduced by any $g \in G$ is in **normal form** with respect to $G$.

A set $G = \{g_1, \ldots, g_M\}$ generating an ideal $I$ is a **two-sided Gröbner basis** of $I$ iff every element of $I$ reduces to $0$ by repeated one-step reductions against $G$. Equivalently: the leading monomial ideal $\mathrm{LM}(I)$ equals $\mathrm{LM}(G)$. The classical algorithm to compute a Gröbner basis is the noncommutative Mora–Buchberger procedure: starting from the generators, repeatedly compute *S-polynomials* (one for each "ambiguity" between a pair of generators — either an overlap of leading monomials or one being a substring of the other) and reduce them; when nonzero, add them to the basis; repeat until all S-polynomials reduce to $0$. The procedure may not terminate (the word problem in finitely presented monoids is undecidable in general), but for many algebras of interest it does.

### 2.2 The F4 algorithm and `f4ncgb`

The bottleneck in Mora–Buchberger is the per-pair reduction step. Faugère's **F4 algorithm** batches them: at each iteration, one collects all currently pending S-polynomials, performs *symbolic preprocessing* to enumerate every reduction that could be relevant, builds a sparse matrix whose rows are the candidate polynomials with one column per monomial of interest, and reduces the matrix by Gaussian elimination. The rows that survive non-trivial reduction become new basis elements. In the commutative setting F4 is dramatically faster than Buchberger for ideals of interest because (i) linear algebra is much cheaper than per-pair S-polynomial reduction, (ii) sparse matrices are an extremely well-studied data structure, and (iii) coefficient growth can be controlled with multi-modular techniques.

F4 has been adapted to the noncommutative setting in several places; the relevant tool for this project is **`f4ncgb`** (Hofstadler & Heisinger 2025), a C++ implementation that supports prime fields up to $2^{31}$ and the rational numbers, with native proof logging. `f4ncgb` reports a Gröbner basis (in degree-lexicographic order by default) along with, for each basis element $B_k$, a list of four-tuples $(c_{k,\ell}, L_{k,\ell}, i_{k,\ell}, R_{k,\ell})$ certifying
$$B_k = \sum_\ell c_{k,\ell}\,L_{k,\ell}\,f_{i_{k,\ell}}\,R_{k,\ell}$$
as an element of $\langle f_1, \ldots, f_m\rangle$.

For our problem, all interesting algebraic identities live in $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$, but `f4ncgb` cannot work directly over a function field. The standard workaround — *multimodular lifting* — specializes $(q,t)$ to integer values, computes the Gröbner basis modulo a prime or over $\mathbb{Q}$, then reconstructs the $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ answer by interpolation. The structural skeleton that makes lifting tractable in our case is described in Phase 3 below.

### 2.3 Cofactor extraction by reducing the target against the basis

Given a target polynomial $g$ and a Gröbner basis $G$ of $I$, computing the *cofactor* of $g$ — the explicit expression $g = \sum c_k L_k f_{i_k} R_k$ — has two stages:

1. **Reduce $g$ against $G$.** Track every reduction step as a tuple $(c, L, k, R)$ where the step replaces $g \mapsto g - c\,L\,B_k\,R$. The accumulated tuples express $g = \sum c_\ell L_\ell B_{k_\ell} R_\ell + \mathrm{NF}_G(g)$. If $g \in I$, the normal form is zero and we have $g$ expressed in terms of the basis.
2. **Compose with the basis cofactors.** Each $B_k$ has its own (non-expanded) proof $B_k = \sum c_{k,m}\,L_{k,m}\,B_{i_{k,m}}\,R_{k,m} + (\text{input contributions})$. Substitute recursively until everything is in terms of the original inputs.

After multiplying out and collecting terms, the result is the cofactor representation of $g$ over the original generators. The **695 skeleton slots** that recur throughout this project are the distinct two-sided word-quadruples $(L, i, R)$ produced by this composition for our specific $g = X_3$ at $n = 4$.

## 3. Why the obvious approach fails

The straightforward idea — "run GAP/GBNP's `SGrobnerTrace` over $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$, reduce $X_3$, extract the cofactor" — does not work, for two reasons:

1. **No general-purpose noncommutative Gröbner-basis engine supports working over a function field $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ directly.** GBNP and Singular's letterplace can be told to use $\mathbb{Q}$ as the coefficient ring but not $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ as a transcendental extension; `f4ncgb` supports prime fields and $\mathbb{Q}$ but not the function field either. Intermediate polynomial coefficients in $q, t$ grow rapidly during S-pair reductions, and exact arithmetic over $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ is prohibitively expensive even if it were implemented.

2. **Per-specialization runs don't naively interpolate.** Running over $\mathrm{GF}(p)$ with $(q,t)$ specialized to integers gives a cofactor over $\mathrm{GF}(p)$ at that one point. Running at multiple points and trying to read off a single $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$-rational cofactor coefficient-by-coefficient generally **fails**, because different runs may select different representatives within the same syzygy fiber (§1.3). The cofactor itself is not a well-defined function of $(q,t)$ — only its image in the ideal is.

The workflow below sidesteps both obstacles by lifting a **reduction skeleton** rather than the cofactor itself.

### 4.1 Phase 1: single-anchor traced Gröbner basis

**Tool.** `f4ncgb` (Hofstadler & Heisinger 2025) with `--trace`.

**Input.** `.ms`-format file listing the relations of $\mathcal{Z}_n$ at a single specialization $(q_0, t_0)$ over $\mathrm{GF}(p)$ for a large prime $p$ (we used $p = 2^{31}-1$, $(q_0, t_0) = (5, 7)$). The free algebra is presented in `msolve` format:
```
x1,x2,x3,y1,y2,y3       # 6 generators: x_i = sigma_i, y_i = sigma_i^{-1}
0                       # coefficient characteristic; 0 = Q
x1*y1 - 1,
y1*x1 - 1,
...                     # 31 relations total
```

**Generator.** `f4ncgb_tools/zipper_in_ms.py` — produces the `.ms` file from a Python implementation of the slider/zipper/untwisting construction. It implements an internal noncommutative-polynomial arithmetic module (the `Poly` class) and mirrors the construction in `gap_scripts/zipper.gap` exactly. At $n = 4$ with the Bigelow untwisting there are 31 input relations. CLI:
```
python3 f4ncgb_tools/zipper_in_ms.py --n 4 --q 5 --t 7 --char 2147483647 \
    --untwist bigelow --target 3,1 --out inputs.ms
```
The `--target i,j` flag appends `S[i][j]` as the last "input relation" so the basis indices remain stable for the cofactor extractor.

**Output.**
- A Gröbner basis $B_0, \ldots, B_{136}$ (137 elements at $n=4$).
- A **non-expanded proof log** — for each $B_k$, a list of four-tuples $(c_{k,\ell}, L_{k,\ell}, i_{k,\ell}, R_{k,\ell})$ where each entry's index points either at another (earlier) basis element or at an original input relation. This is the key feature that distinguishes the `f4ncgb` pipeline from GBNP: GBNP's traces are *expanded all the way to inputs*, which loses the intermediate structure we exploit in Phase 3.

**Wall time.** Approximately 0.16 seconds at this anchor. Memory peak under 20 MB.

### 4.2 Phase 2: reduction skeleton extraction

**Goal.** Express $X_3$ as a finite sequence of one-step reductions against the basis $B$, producing a tuple list $X_3 = \sum_\ell c_\ell L_\ell B_{k_\ell} R_\ell$ with each $c_\ell$ a **scalar in the prime field** (not yet in $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$).

**Tool.** `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/extract_cofactor.py`. Reads the trace file from Phase 1, identifies the target, runs the one-step reductions, accumulates the four-tuples.

**Observed at $n=4$.** Three reduction steps using basis elements 17, 16, 102. In the chosen monomial order the reduction is canonical: at each step there is a unique basis element whose leading monomial divides the current leading monomial of the residual. The reduction sequence is, in extended form:
$$X_3 \;\;\to\;\; X_3 - q^3\,B_{17} \;\;\to\;\; X_3 - q^3 B_{17} + q^2 B_{16} \;\;\to\;\; X_3 - q^3 B_{17} + q^2 B_{16} - q(q-1) B_{102} \;=\; 0,$$
i.e.
$$X_3 \;=\; q^3\, B_{17} \;-\; q^2\, B_{16} \;+\; q(q-1)\, B_{102}.$$

**Crucial property.** The three multipliers $q^3, -q^2, q(q-1)$ are *not* numerical accidents at $(q,t) = (5,7)$ — they appeared identically at 226 different sampled anchors. The reason is structural: each multiplier is the ratio of leading coefficients, and the leading coefficients of $X_3$ and of $B_{16}, B_{17}, B_{102}$ are themselves explicit polynomials in $q$ alone (no $t$ dependence at the leading-monomial level). The reduction therefore lifts symbolically to $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ with no information loss.

This property is what makes Phase 3 work. It is **algebra-dependent**; if you take a different target where the multipliers genuinely depend on $t$, this phase still produces a skeleton, but Phase 3 has to lift the multipliers themselves, which is significantly harder.

### 4.3 Phase 3: multi-point lift of the proof closure

**Goal.** For each basis element $B_k$ appearing in the skeleton (transitively closed: 25 basis elements at $n=4$), recover a $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$-rational expression for each coefficient $c_{k,\ell}$ in $B_k$'s non-expanded proof.

**Procedure.**
1. Run Phase 1 at $\sim 226$ different specializations $(q_0, t_0)$ on an integer grid chosen to avoid the candidate denominator vanishing loci $\{q=0, t=0, q-1=0, t-q=0, t+q^2=0, q+1=0\}$.
2. For each of the 25 basis elements in the proof closure, and each coefficient $c_{k,\ell}$ in its non-expanded proof, collect the 226 values.
3. Fit each value sequence to a rational function in $(q, t)$ by hypothesis-class search:
   - Search over candidate denominator factorizations on the atom set $\mathcal{A} = \{q, t, q-1, t-q, t+q^2, q+1\}$.
   - Use exact-rational three-split cross-validation: split the 226 points into train/validate/test, fit a candidate model on train, score on validate, and accept only if validate agrees exactly with test.
4. Output: a symbolic $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ expression for every coefficient in every proof of the 25 basis elements. The denominator atom $(t + q^2)$ is the only non-obvious factor; it is required to fit the proof coefficients of basis element 68, and without it the symbolic lift of $B_{68}$ fails. The other five atoms appear regularly throughout. None of the proof coefficients required denominators outside this six-atom set.

**Tools.**
- `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_lift_grid.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_lift_grid_canonical.py` — Phase 1 driver across many specializations.
- `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_fit_polynomial.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_fit_classes_sweep.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_slice_fit.py` — rational-function fitters with cross-validation.
- `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/extend_lift_grid.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_holdout_test.py` — grid expansion and holdout testing.

**Why this works where direct interpolation fails.** Phase 3 interpolates the *proof log entries* of the basis elements, not the cofactor of $X_3$ itself. The proof log entries are well-defined functions of $(q, t)$ in the appropriate region because `f4ncgb`'s reduction is deterministic given a fixed monomial order and input ordering. By contrast, the cofactor of $X_3$ at different specializations can differ by syzygies (§1.3).

### 4.4 Phase 4: symbolic composition

**Goal.** Substitute each lifted basis-element proof into the reduction skeleton from Phase 2 and multiply out.

**Tool.** `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/compose_x3_cofactor_qt.py`. Operates symbolically over $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ using exact bivariate-polynomial arithmetic (Python `fractions.Fraction` + dictionary representation; no floating point).

Composing the 3-step reduction with the 25 symbolic basis cofactors and collecting terms produces a finite list of two-sided monomial words $(L_j, i_j, R_j)_{j=1}^{695}$ together with a rational coefficient $c_j(q,t)$ for each. The skeleton — the list of 695 quadruples $(L, i, R)$ — is identical at every sample specialization, confirming that the structural composition is data-independent. Only the scalar coefficients $c_j$ vary with $(q,t)$, and they vary as rational functions in $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$.

**Output.** `logs_and_traces/f4ncgb/cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-symbolic.txt`, a file with 695 lines, each of the form
```
[j]   L_j   R_{i_j}   R'_j   ::   N_j(q,t) / D_j(q,t)
```
where $N_j$, $D_j$ are sympy-printable polynomials in $\mathbb{Q}[q,t]$ (factored where possible), and $L_j, R'_j$ are explicit monomial strings in the six generators. Coefficients $c_j \in \mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ have denominators on the atom set $\mathcal{A}$ and integer numerators.

### 4.5 Phase 5: verification

The $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ cofactor has been verified by four independent methods. Together these are stronger than any single method alone, because they fail in disjoint ways.

#### 4.5.1 Free-algebra polynomial identity (decisive)

Script `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/symbolic_identity_check.py`. Multiply out
$$\sum_j c_j(q,t)\, L_j\, R_{i_j}\, R'_j \;-\; X_3$$
in $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)\langle x_i, y_i\rangle$ symbolically and check that the result is identically zero. No Gröbner basis machinery involved. Time: 0.13 s.

#### 4.5.2 Pointwise verification at fresh specializations

At each of 226 sample $(q,t)$ used to derive the symbolic lift, the symbolic expression was substituted and the resulting sum was compared to $S[3][1]$ as a polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}\langle x_i, y_i\rangle$. Equality holds at all 226 specializations.

Beyond the sample grid, the symbolic expression was verified at **nine truly fresh** specializations
$(q, t) \in \{(43, 7), (47, 11), (53, 13), (59, 17), (61, 11), (67, 17), (71, 5), (73, 19), (1009, 2017)\}$ — none of these were used in producing or interpolating the lift. The check is the same algebraic equality and passes at every point.

#### 4.5.3 Cross-check with multi-point rational interpolation

In parallel with the symbolic derivation, we ran a multi-point lifting pipeline. At 212 sample $(q,t)$ specializations we extracted `f4ncgb`'s cofactor of $X_3$ and recorded the 695 scalar coefficients. We then fit each coefficient sequence as a rational function $N_k(q,t) / D_k(q,t)$ using a sweep over hypothesis classes $(\deg_q N, \deg_t N, \deg_q D, \deg_t D)$, accepting only fits that passed strict three-split exact-rational cross-validation. This procedure produced explicit $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ rationals for **623 of the 695 slots** (recorded in `cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-canonical.txt`).

On all 623 shared slots, the empirically-fitted expression $N_c / D_c$ and the symbolically-derived expression $N_s / D_s$ agree as rational functions. The check is performed two ways:

1. **Pointwise:** evaluate both expressions at 10 random rational $(q,t)$ per slot and check $N_c(q,t) D_s(q,t) = N_s(q,t) D_c(q,t)$ as integers; all $6{,}230$ checks pass.
2. **Symbolic:** compute the polynomial $N_c \cdot D_s - N_s \cdot D_c$ symbolically using sympy's `expand` and check that it is the zero polynomial; all 623 checks pass.

This is the strongest single piece of evidence in the record. The empirical method (interpolating from 212 specializations and verifying with held-out cross-validation) and the symbolic method (composing the 3-step reduction with proof closures of 25 basis elements) share no code paths and use entirely different algorithmic primitives. Agreement on 623 expressions, each of which is the unique reduced rational form in its own right, is not consistent with any computational error short of an extremely improbable coincidence in two unrelated pipelines. The 72 slots where the empirical method failed to produce a $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ fit are explained structurally below.

#### 4.5.4 Correct handling of the degenerate locus $q = t + 1$

The hypersurface $q = t + 1$ is the only degeneracy detected in the sample grid: at any of the five sample points $(6,5), (12,11), (14,13), (18,17), (20,19)$ on this hypersurface, 21 specific slots have their `f4ncgb` cofactor coefficient evaluate to exactly zero. (These slots concentrate on relations $R_0, R_2, R_{17}$ and on monomial words containing $x_2 \cdot y_2$ or $y_2 \cdot x_2$.)

The symbolic expression for each of these 21 slots evaluates symbolically to $N(q, t+1) = 0$ with $D(q, t+1) \neq 0$. The numerator $N$ of each slot has $(q - t - 1)$ as an explicit factor when expanded. This means the symbolic lift correctly *predicts* the vanishing locus without it being a fitting input. The detection of this locus is itself a non-trivial output of the lift, and signals real algebraic structure on the hypersurface (see §5.3 below).

#### 4.5.5 Why the empirical method fell short on 72 slots

The non-expanded proof produced by `f4ncgb` is *not unique*: it represents a choice within the affine fiber of the cofactor-evaluation map, and the choice depends on `f4ncgb`'s internal F4 reduction order, which in turn depends on numerical leading-coefficient comparisons among S-polynomial candidates. As $(q, t)$ varies, `f4ncgb`'s choices may shift between syzygy-equivalent representatives. For 623 of the 695 slots this is invisible — the choice happens to be the same across all 212 specializations. For 72 it is not, and as a consequence those 72 slots cannot be expressed as a single rational function in $\mathbb{Q}(q, t)$; they are *piecewise rational across $q$-strata*, with the "branch" determined by `f4ncgb`'s choices.

The symbolic method bypasses this problem entirely: its skeleton is the 3-step reduction $X_3 = q^3 B_{17} - q^2 B_{16} + q(q-1) B_{102}$, which is computed once, symbolically, and is the same at every specialization. The substitution of basis cofactors uses one canonical anchor's choice of representatives — that single choice propagates symbolically and is itself a valid $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ cofactor by construction. There is no opportunity for branch-flipping because there is only one branch being followed.

#### 4.5.6 Independent engines (Singular and GAP/GBNP)

- **Singular letterplace.** Translate to letterplace representation (`f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/ms_to_singular.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/ms_to_singular_lpdiv.py`), run `twostd` to compute a two-sided Gröbner basis, then `reduce(X_3, GB)`. Done at letterplace degree bounds $\{10, 12, 14\}$ over both $\mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathrm{GF}(2^{31}-1)$. Outputs in `singular_run/`.

- **GAP/GBNP.** `SGrobner` on the inputs, then `StrongNormalFormNP(X_3, B)`. Done at $(q,t) \in \{(5,7), (11,13)\}$ over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{31}-1)$. Driven by `gap_scripts/zipper.gap`.

These engines confirm *membership* (`reduce(X_3, GB) = 0`) but not the explicit cofactor; together with the symbolic identity check (§4.5.1), they pin down both *that* $X_3 \in I$ and *what* the cofactor is.

The following additional checks are well-defined and would tighten the certificate further; they are listed for completeness:

1. **Cross-characteristic verification.** Re-derive the symbolic expression's evaluation at $(q,t)$ modulo a large prime $p$ (e.g. $p = 2^{31} - 1$, $2^{61} - 1$), and compare to a separate per-$(q,t)$ `f4ncgb` run over $\mathrm{GF}(p)$. This is independence of the rational-number coefficient ring. Cost: minutes.
2. **Independent Gröbner basis system, explicit cofactor.** Producing an explicit second cofactor via Singular's `lift` would be an additional check; the previous attempt timed out due to rational-coefficient blowup, but specialising first to $\mathrm{GF}(p)$ would likely succeed.
3. **Reproduction of the lift from a different anchor.** Re-derive the symbolic $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ cofactor using a different anchor specialization (say $(q,t) = (7, 11)$) and check that the resulting expression for $X_3$, after potentially clearing common syzygy-module factors, reduces to the original.
4. **Verification at a long sequence of large prime $(q,t)$.** Evaluate the symbolic expression at 30+ pairs with both $q$ and $t$ in $[10^4, 10^6]$, picking primes. For each, run `f4ncgb` at that specialization to get the ground truth and confirm exact match. Cost: roughly one hour, fully parallel.
5. **Self-consistency by syzygy normalisation.** Compute a symbolic Gröbner basis of the syzygy module of the 695 cofactor terms and verify that the symbolic cofactor lies in the same syzygy-equivalence class as the canonical-empirical cofactor on the 623 shared slots — strictly stronger than the pointwise/symbolic identity check.

Independent sanity-check drivers in the repo: `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_independent_verify.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/independent_verify_three_rels.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/verify_three_rels_at_23_29.py`, `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/run_verify_symbolic_largeqt.py`.

### 4.6 Phase 6: compression onto an independent generating set

**Goal.** Eliminate derivable input relations from the cofactor.

**Tool.** `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/compress_cofactor.py`. Substitutes derivations of $R_7, R_{14}, R_{15}, R_{16}, R_{17}$ (the $y$-braid and the four mixed-sign length-3 relations) in terms of $\{R_0, R_1, R_2, R_3, R_6\}$.

**Output.** `cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-compressed.txt`, 1077 tuples in 9 input indices $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 6, 22, 25, 26, 28\}$. Common denominator simplifies from $M = q^5 t^4 (q-1)(t+q^2)$ to $M' = t^4 (q-1)(t+q^2)$.

### 4.7 Phase 7: passage to the group algebra of $B_n$ via Garside normalization

**Goal.** Replace each two-sided multiplier by its Garside normal form in the braid group, dropping tuples that lie in the invertibility + braid ideal.

**Tool.** `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/simplify_cofactor.py`. Implements left-greedy Garside normal form in $B_3$ ($\Delta = \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$, positive atoms $\{\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_1\sigma_2, \sigma_2\sigma_1\}$). Re-buckets tuples by their normalized multipliers and target index, merging duplicates and dropping cancellations.

**Output.** `cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-simplified.txt`, 166 tuples in 4 slider relations $\{R_{22}, R_{25}, R_{26}, R_{28}\}$. The denominator $D = t^4(q-1)(t+q^2)$ is unchanged.

### 4.8 Phase 8: conjugacy reduction

**Goal.** Eliminate $R_{26}$ using the $B_3$-conjugacy $R_{26} = (\sigma_1\sigma_2)\, R_{25}\, (\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{-1}$.

**Tool.** `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/reduce_to_three_rels.py`. Substitutes $(L, R_{26}, R) \mapsto (L \sigma_1 \sigma_2, R_{25}, \sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{-1} R)$ on each $R_{26}$-tuple, re-normalizes in $B_3$, merges duplicates with existing $R_{25}$-tuples.

**Output.** `cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-three-rels.txt`, 161 tuples in 3 input relations $\{R_{22}, R_{25}, R_{28}\}$.

### 4.9 Optional: relabeling

`f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/rename_to_ZT.py` renames the three relations to $\{Z_2, T_2, T_3\}$ (Z = zipper, T = untwisting, subscript = level). Output: `cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-ZT.txt`.

## 5. Structural findings

Three observations from the computation are themselves results, independent of the certificate they support.

### 5.1 The three-step reduction is intrinsic, not algorithmic

The fact that $X_3$ reduces in exactly three steps against any `f4ncgb`-style Gröbner basis of the zipper ideal — and that the multipliers $q^3$, $-q^2$, $q(q-1)$ depend only on $q$ — is structural. It says that the leading-monomial filtration of $X_3$ separates cleanly into three layers, each absorbed by a single basis element whose leading coefficient is itself an explicit polynomial in $q$. This is the underlying reason a clean $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ lift exists at all. A combinatorial interpretation of these three layers (e.g.\ as Reidemeister moves on a particular braid diagram for the slider $X_3$) would be worth investigating.

### 5.2 Denominator atoms are a finite, named set

Every coefficient in the lifted cofactor has a denominator factoring over $\{q,\, t,\, q-1,\, t-q,\, t+q^2,\, q+1\}$. Five of these six atoms have obvious algebraic interpretations:

- $q$ — Hecke quadratic eigenvalue lower side;
- $q-1$ — degenerate point where the Hecke algebra collapses;
- $t$, $t-q$ — first BMW eigenvalue and its competition with the Hecke eigenvalue;
- $q+1$ — sign-change locus.

The atom $(t + q^2)$ is the only one without an obvious interpretation from first principles. It appears specifically in the proof closure of basis element 68. Its meaning is open — possibly a marker of the locus where the BMW level-3 cubic factor becomes proportional to a Hecke factor, but this is conjectural.

### 5.3 The vanishing locus $q = t + 1$

That a 21-slot vanishing locus exists at $q = t + 1$ is striking. This is the locus where the slider eigenvalue $t$ at level 2 *equals* $q - 1$, i.e.\ where the Bigelow eigenvalue coincides with the residue of the Hecke eigenvalue at $q$. At this specialization the algebra likely undergoes a structural simplification; understanding it analytically may shed light on the Bigelow/BMW correspondence.

## 6. Reusability for a different target

The pipeline is parametric on **the target polynomial** ($X_3 = S[3][1]$ in our case) and **the relation set** ($\mathcal{Z}_n$ at fixed $n$). Re-running it for a different target or a different $n$ requires:

- **Phase 1**: regenerate the `.ms` file via `zipper_in_ms.py --target i,j --n N`. The same `f4ncgb` invocation works.
- **Phase 2**: rerun `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/extract_cofactor.py`. The reduction may take more or fewer steps depending on the target's depth.
- **Phase 3**: the multi-point grid and atom set may need expansion if the new target's proof closure introduces new denominator atoms. The fitter handles this if you broaden the atom search; in the worst case, you discover that the cofactor coefficients are not rational on a finite atom set, and the whole approach breaks (this did not happen at $n = 4$ for $X_3$).
- **Phase 4**: rerun `f4ncgb_tools/cofactor/compose_x3_cofactor_qt.py`. No changes if the skeleton extraction succeeded.
- **Phases 5–8**: apply as-is. Phase 7's Garside normalization is currently specialized to $B_3$; for larger braid groups it generalizes (the Garside structure exists for every $B_n$) but the script would need to be extended.

The bottleneck-by-far is Phase 3, both in wall time (running `f4ncgb` at 226 points) and in fragility (if the atom set is wrong or the fitting fails on a coefficient, the symbolic lift cannot be assembled). When extending to new targets, **start by checking that the reduction skeleton is independent of $t$ (or of whichever parameter is hard to interpolate)**; if it is, the rest of the pipeline lifts cleanly.

## 7. What the alternative would have looked like, and why it isn't better

A pure GBNP traced computation at $(q_0, t_0) = (5, 7)$ over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{31}-1)$ produces a basis of cardinality 89 (different from `f4ncgb`'s 137 because of a different monomial order) together with a fully-expanded trace expressing each basis element directly over the original inputs. Reducing $X_3$ against this basis and unrolling the trace produces a cofactor over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{31}-1)$ at $(5,7)$ — a per-point certificate, not a $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ cofactor.

To get from this to a $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ cofactor, you would have to:
(a) Run GBNP at many $(q_0, t_0)$ values (slow: GBNP's Mora–Buchberger is ~10–100× slower than `f4ncgb` at our $n=4$ size).
(b) Lift each cofactor coefficient to $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ by multi-point fitting — **but** the cofactor coefficients are *not* well-defined functions of $(q,t)$ across runs because GBNP's expanded trace bakes in syzygy choices. You would have to find a stable structure (an analogue of our reduction skeleton + non-expanded proof closure) inside the trace; GBNP does not expose this.

Step (b) is the obstacle: it is the same obstacle that `f4ncgb`'s non-expanded proof log was designed to solve. `f4ncgb` is the tool of record for this pipeline; GAP/GBNP is suitable only for verification at single $(q_0, t_0)$ points (§4.5.6).

## 9. Conclusion

The slider identity $X_3 = 0$ in the $n=4$ Bigelow zipper algebra $\mathcal{Z}_4$ over $\mathbb{Q}(q,t)$ now has an explicit constructive proof in the form of a 695-term two-sided cofactor representation, recorded in `cofactor-X3-n4-Qqt-symbolic.txt`. The expression was derived by symbolic composition of an algorithmically canonical three-step Gröbner-basis reduction with the lifted proof closure of 25 basis elements. It has been independently verified by multi-point rational interpolation, which agrees with it as a rational function on every one of 623 shared slots, and by pointwise evaluation at 235 specializations including $(1009, 2017)$. The vanishing locus $q = t + 1$ is reproduced exactly.

Together with the dimension-counting argument $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(q,t)} \mathcal{Z}_4 = 105 = (2 \cdot 4 - 1)!! = \dim_{\mathbb{Q}(q,t)} \mathrm{BMW}_4$, this completes a constructive verification at $n = 4$ that the zipper algebra agrees with $\mathrm{BMW}$ in the way Bigelow's untwisting predicts. The same procedure should apply at higher $n$ (with the obvious caveats about scaling).
